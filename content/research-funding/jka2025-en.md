# JKA Research Grant (FY2025)

## Current Project

### 2025M-424

- **Project Title**: DX Welfare Care Supporting the 100-Year Life Era: Privacy-Preserving Elderly Monitoring and Health Support System (FY2025)
- **Recipient**: University of Miyazaki
- **Principal Investigator**: Thi Thi Zin (University of Miyazaki, Faculty of Engineering)

## Research Overview

This project aims to develop a privacy-preserving elderly monitoring and health support system to enhance the safety of older adults and reduce the burden on caregivers in Japan's rapidly aging society. By utilizing advanced contactless technologies, the system analyzes elderly behavior and gait information, enabling the detection of abnormal actions and fall risks.

## Background

In 2023, Japan faced a shortage of approximately 180,000 care workers compared with the number required by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare. This shortage has led to declining care service quality, longer waiting times for users, and increased workloads for caregivers. To address these challenges, there is a critical need for efficient monitoring systems that operate continuously without relying on human observation while protecting privacy through depth-camera technology and advanced image-processing techniques.

This research develops a system that uses depth cameras and human pose estimation technology to identify abnormal behavior among hospitalized elderly patients in a non-contact and privacy-preserving manner. The automated monitoring functions aim to improve patient safety, reduce caregiver burden, enhance healthcare quality, and protect privacy, thereby addressing significant challenges in medical and nursing-care settings.

## Research Contents

- **Research on Elderly Behavior Estimation and Risk Behavior Detection** A non-contact data collection environment was established using depth cameras. A spatiotemporal model integrating Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) and Transformers classified four activity states; standing, sitting, lying, and transitioning. A monitoring application was also developed, enabling observation from locations such as nursing stations. [Read More](https://www.cc.miyazaki-u.ac.jp/imagelab/research/projects/detail/elderly-monitoring)

  <br>

- **Research on Gait Information Acquisition and Fall Risk Assessment** Detailed feature analysis was conducted on five elderly participants in their 80s who attended the facility regularly. To preserve privacy, only skeletal information was analyzed. Twelve gait-related features were quantified. A three-level fall-risk assessment based on body-axis tilt angle (Safe/Caution/Dangerous) was developed, along with a comprehensive gait score integrating all twelve gait features. This enabled classification of walking conditions into four grades (A–D). [Read More](https://www.cc.miyazaki-u.ac.jp/imagelab/research/projects/detail/gait-extraction)

## Contribution to Society

The developed system achieves 24-hour automated monitoring while protecting privacy by analyzing only depth images and skeletal information without identifying individuals. It can directly contribute to accident prevention through early detection of falls, bed-exit events, and abnormal postures in hospitals, nursing facilities, and home-care settings.

Quantitative health assessment using gait indicators can support rehabilitation evaluation and preventive interventions for fall risk, thereby improving the quality of healthcare and caregiving. Combined with long-term person re-identification technologies, the system can continuously monitor individual health trends and support data-driven care planning.
